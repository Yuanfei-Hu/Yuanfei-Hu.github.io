Memory consolidation and replay in continuous-attractor networks

Does sleep replay simply strengthen memories, or can replay timing and neural overlap determine whether similar memories blend together or become easier to distinguish?

Continuous attractor networks
Neural fields
Circular statistics
Simulation-based experiments
Bootstrap and permutation inference
MATLAB · Python

Code and materials ↗

Approach

I developed a label-free computational model of sleep replay on continuous memory representations and designed a 4,032-row simulation study with matched controls. The study examined how replay timing and representational overlap altered the separation and recall of nearby memories.

Findings

Within a bounded parameter regime, replay separated previously confused memories by 0.0517 radians and reduced recall error by 0.00710 radians. I conducted a locked replication across 48 simulated subjects and tested recovery under sparse and noisy model variants.

Further analysis

I developed a biologically constrained excitatory/inhibitory implementation, ran parameter sweeps, bootstrap and permutation inference, held-out prediction, and reanalysed two public targeted-memory-reactivation datasets.

Limitations

The reported separation occurs in a bounded model regime. The behavioural predictions can be compared with existing data, but biological claims about replay mechanisms still require physiological validation.
