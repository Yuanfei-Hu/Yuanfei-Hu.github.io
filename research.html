Research

Memory, dynamics,
and adaptive behavior.

I am interested in how the state of a system changes what it can remember and how it behaves. In neural systems, this leads me to questions about persistent activity, replay, interference, and variability. In artificial systems, I am interested in whether related principles can support adaptation without sacrificing interpretability.

My background in applied mathematics shapes how I approach these questions: I usually begin with a mechanism, formalize it as a dynamical model, and ask what observations would distinguish it from plausible alternatives.

01

Neural dynamics and memory

How do recurrent population dynamics maintain a memory, allow it to drift, or transform it through replay? My current work uses continuous attractor and neural-field models to study how timing and overlap can change the relationship between similar memories.

Continuous attractors · neural fields · circular statistics · simulation

02

Adaptive computation

I am interested in biological ideas that are often compressed into a single reward signal in machine learning: salience, surprise, homeostasis, replay, and internally generated priorities. The question for me is not whether an artificial agent can imitate a brain, but which mechanisms are useful and testable.

NeuroAI · intrinsic signals · replay · adaptive agents

03

Geometry and system structure

Symmetry and invariance provide a way to encode what should remain unchanged when coordinates or observations change. I use these ideas to ask whether model structure can make learned systems easier to monitor, test, and interpret.

Equivariance · dynamical systems · anomaly detection · validation

Questions I am thinking about

Current questions

  1. When does replay differentiate overlapping memories, and when does it increase interference?
  2. Can internal signals such as surprise or homeostatic error determine what an agent should revisit or learn from?
  3. How can symmetry and dynamical consistency be used as testable constraints rather than decorative inductive biases?
  4. Which conclusions are supported by behavior alone, and which require neural or physiological evidence?
